Takeaways (visual read)

Not a survey — a plain-language look at these frames only.

A private house lot on the coastal terrace, set back from the bluff — these tiles are useful for comparing the house footprint, paths, and beach/bluff edge over time, not for hazard conclusions.

  • 1985 is an early black-and-white / low-resolution tile. Structures and the bluff edge are hard to read at this zoom; treat it as a coarse baseline, not a detailed site plan.
  • By the mid-2000s color frames (e.g. 2004–2005), the dark multi-gabled roof of the house is clear, inland from the bluff with a driveway toward the Osprey Reach cul-de-sac and light paths through the coastal grass/scrub.
  • Across 2012–2024, the same general layout holds: house on the terrace, walking paths toward and along the bluff, sandy beach and surf to the west. Neighbor roofs and the road loop stay useful fixed references.
  • Beach width, foam, and how “rocky” vs sandy the shore looks change a lot tile to tile — tide, season, and sensor matter as much as any real shoreline move at this resolution.
  • Nothing in these frames jumps out as a dramatic, sudden bluff failure into the building footprint. Small year-to-year edge changes would be easy to miss (or over-read) here.

Desk read of Google Earth Pro historical tiles only — not geologic, survey, or insurance advice. Tide, season, vegetation, and imagery source can change appearance without a real ground change.
